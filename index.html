01 · Executive summary

Every drone thinks locally.
The network understands globally.

NEXUS AI is a proposed distributed intelligent drone network built around one idea: resilience should be designed in from the first line of code, not added after the first failure.

Each drone carries onboard computing and AI to process its own sensor information, monitor its condition and exchange relevant information with other network members. A central coordinating AI maintains a shared system view, while backup and temporary local coordinators keep the network operating when devices or communication links become unavailable.

A remote human administrator uses one command interface to monitor drone locations, environmental observations, network health and system alerts. The design emphasises human supervision, reliable information sharing, fault tolerance, cybersecurity and safe operation. The initial development focus is civilian monitoring and response: search and rescue, wildfire observation, flood assessment and environmental mapping.

Core principle

Every drone contributes local intelligence, while the connected system builds one shared understanding of the monitored environment.

Project objectives

07 measurable goals
  1. 01Process sensor data locally on every drone.
  2. 02Exchange status and observations over available wireless links.
  3. 03Route around a lost direct connection through alternative paths.
  4. 04Coordinate centrally, locally and through a backup — no single point of failure.
  5. 05Maintain a shared map with timestamps and confidence indicators.
  6. 06Give one administrator a unified view of health, alerts and missions.
  7. 07Protect the system with identity, encryption, validation and audit trails.

02 · The four main components

Four layers.
No single point of failure.

Intelligence at the edge, communication through the mesh, coordination across the fleet and a single accountable human interface. Select a layer to explore its responsibilities.

Every layer degrades safely. No layer is required for basic survival.
3.1 — EDGE INTELLIGENCEONBOARD

Individual AI drones

Each drone is an intelligent network member rather than a simple remote-controlled device. Its onboard AI processes camera and sensor information, monitors battery and hardware status, keeps local records and exchanges what matters with authorised members.

  • Local processing of camera and sensor observations.
  • Health monitoring for battery, hardware and communication status.
  • Local storage for observations that cannot be synchronised immediately.
  • Communication with nearby members using approved protocols.
  • Predefined safe behaviour when operator or network connectivity is lost.
  • Synchronisation of buffered observations when a valid connection returns.
Autonomy-firstStore & forwardSafe by default
3.2 — MESH SYSTEMSELF-FORMING

A network that routes around damage

Drones form wireless links with neighbours and move messages across multiple hops. When a direct path disappears, the mesh looks for another authenticated route — or stores the message until one becomes available.

  • Self-forming topology with no fixed infrastructure required.
  • Multi-hop routing that avoids failed nodes and blocked sectors.
  • Store-and-forward buffering for intermittent connectivity.
  • Continuous link-quality monitoring and neighbour discovery.
  • Authenticated, encrypted device-to-device communication.
  • Graceful partition and reconnection instead of network-wide failure.
Multi-hopLink-awareSelf-healing
3.3 — DYNAMIC COORDINATIONFAULT-TOLERANT

Central, backup and local

A Main AI maintains shared state while a Backup AI keeps a ready replica. If a group loses its central path, the group elects a temporary local coordinator and keeps operating until reconnection.

  • Main AI owns the shared operational view and global tasking.
  • Backup AI replicates coordinator state and monitors readiness.
  • Controlled handover after a health check — never a race for leadership.
  • Temporary local coordinators elected inside separated groups.
  • Conflict resolution using timestamps, source identity and version data.
  • Split-brain prevention and a complete, auditable decision trail.
Shared stateHandoverLocal leaders
3.4 — ADMIN INTERFACEHUMAN-LED

One view for the human in charge

The remote administrator sees the fleet as a connected system: live positions, observation freshness, network health and alerts in one place. Important mission decisions stay in human hands.

  • Live map of drone positions, observations and network status.
  • Health, battery, connectivity and freshness indicators.
  • Clear separation between confirmed, delayed and stale information.
  • Authorised mission planning with explicit approvals.
  • Alert review, event history and complete audit logs.
  • Role-based access for operators, supervisors and maintainers.
Unified mapExplainableAuditable

    04 · How the system works

    From first handshake
    to continuous health checks.

    Seven steps describe a full operating cycle — and every cycle assumes that something, somewhere, may fail.

    1. 01

      Initialization

      Drones join the network, authenticate and register identity, capabilities and current health.

    2. 02

      Local AI activation

      Each drone begins processing its own sensor information and monitoring its condition.

    3. 03

      Network formation

      Drones establish available wireless links and discover reachable communication paths.

    4. 04

      Observation collection

      Drones collect environmental observations and label them with timestamps and source information.

    5. 05

      Information sync

      Local groups exchange records; the central system combines validated updates into one shared view.

    6. 06

      Remote visualization

      The command center presents the latest shared map, device status and network health to the administrator.

    7. 07

      Continuous health checks

      Connectivity, freshness, battery and hardware are monitored continuously, with predefined safe responses.

    05 · Self-healing communication

    NEXUS AI is designed
    to expect failure.

    A link can disappear, a battery can run low, a drone can be lost. None of those events should automatically make the rest of the network unusable. Run each failure yourself.

    Link failure

    The network marks the route unavailable and seeks another authenticated, usable route.

    Drone disconnected

    The drone follows its predefined safety procedure and preserves local records where possible.

    Drone destroyed or lost

    Information already synchronised to other nodes remains available even if the original drone is lost.

    Drone reconnects

    New records are reconciled with existing data, preserving timestamps and source identity.

    Main AI unavailable

    The backup coordinator can take over after a controlled handover and health check.

    Group separated

    Its local coordinator maintains limited records and synchronisation until reconnection.

    An honest limitation

    No system can guarantee delivery of information that existed only on a device that was destroyed or permanently lost before it could be transmitted or copied. That is why NEXUS AI replicates early and labels every record with its freshness.

    06 · Shared environmental map

    One map.
    Honest about what is live and what is old.

    The shared map is the central output of NEXUS AI. It combines validated observations and device information into one digital representation of the monitored area — with source and time attached to every claim.

    Map layers and their purpose
    Map layerPurpose
    Drone locationsLatest reported position and update time for each drone.
    EnvironmentTerrain, obstacles and mapped features.
    Observed movementObjects or changes, with source and timestamp.
    Historical observationsPrevious records retained for comparison and review.
    Network statusAvailable links, disconnected devices and local groups.
    Drone conditionBattery, hardware and communication health.
    Confidence & freshnessHow reliable and recent each observation is.

    Freshness states

    LiveConfirmed in the last few seconds.
    DelayedRelayed over a slower or multi-hop route.
    StaleLast known position — not confirmed.

    If an object is no longer visible, the interface shows its last observed position and marks it as stale rather than implying the position is still confirmed.

    07 · Storage & synchronization

    The system never depends
    on one device for every record.

    Information is copied toward the centre as connectivity allows. Three tiers keep the shared picture alive even when part of the fleet is on its own.

    TIER 1

    Local storage

    Observations and operational records collected by an individual drone.

    on-drone
    TIER 2

    Regional storage

    Selected records synchronised within a local group and its temporary coordinator.

    group
    TIER 3

    Central storage

    Shared records available to the Main AI, Backup AI and the command center.

    shared map

    Data reconciliation

    After reconnection, records are compared using timestamps, source identity and version information. Newer confirmed data wins; older records remain available for review.

    Priority-aware transfer

    Small status and alert messages are handled separately from large files such as video or map packages, so the essential picture moves first.

    A file is not inherently required for long-distance communication. The distinction is between small messages and larger data transfers; both can use suitable wireless links.

    NEXUS AI is proposed for civilian monitoring and response applications — environmental observation, search and rescue, disaster assessment and safe fleet management. It does not specify weapon systems, coordinated attacks or autonomous targeting.

    09 · Security & reliability

    Trust is designed in,
    not bolted on.

    Identity, authorisation, encryption and a complete audit trail are part of the architecture — not an afterthought for a later phase.

    Encrypted links

    Encrypted communication between authenticated devices.

    Identity & roles

    Unique device identities and role-based access permissions.

    Admin access

    Secure administrator authentication and access logging.

    Data validation

    Validation of data sources and protection against corrupted records.

    Update control

    Software update controls and secure configuration management.

    Safe modes

    Emergency stop, safe-mode and loss-of-link procedures.

    Audit trail

    Audit logs for important system events and administrative actions.

    10 · Potential civilian applications

    Built for the places
    people need eyes the most.

    The same distributed architecture supports very different missions. The common need: current, trustworthy information across a wide area.

    Search & rescue

    Coordinate observations over a wide search area and keep working when the terrain breaks direct links.

    Wildfire monitoring

    Map fire boundaries, smoke and changing conditions with timestamps and source confidence.

    Flood & disaster assessment

    Create updated maps of affected areas and access routes when infrastructure is unavailable.

    Environmental monitoring

    Observe forests, coastlines, wildlife habitats and protected areas over long periods.

    Agriculture

    Monitor large fields and identify areas that need a human inspection, not a guess.

    13–14 · Vision & first prototype

    A reliable distributed
    information system. Not just a fleet.

    NEXUS AI aims to bring individual intelligence, resilient communication, distributed coordination and human supervision into one connected system.

    Every drone contributes local observations; the network shares and synchronises information; central and local coordinators help the system remain available; and the remote command center presents a unified view to authorised human administrators.

    The long-term goal is not simply to operate many drones. It is to build a reliable distributed information system in which many intelligent devices can cooperate, recover from individual failures and help people understand changing environments.

    Think locally Share resiliently Coordinate without a single failure point Keep humans accountable
    Recommended first prototypeLive on this page

    Start with software.

    Begin with a software-only simulation of a small civilian monitoring fleet. Simulate several drones, each with a local AI agent and a unique identity. Add a mesh-style communication model, a central coordinator, a backup coordinator, one temporary local coordinator and a simple map dashboard.

    Then test what happens when links disappear, devices stop responding and connectivity returns. This validates the core architecture before hardware investment.
